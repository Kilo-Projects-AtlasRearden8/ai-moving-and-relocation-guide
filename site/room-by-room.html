Room-by-Room Inventory

An inventory is a list of what you own, room by room. It helps with packing, insurance claims if something is lost or damaged, and deciding what to keep. You do not need to list every sock — big items and anything valuable are enough.

How to inventory

  1. Pick one room. Start small.
  2. Open a notebook, spreadsheet, or your phone notes app.
  3. For each item, write: item name, room, condition, box number (once packed), and an example value placeholder if you want (e.g., “EXAMPLE $0.00” — not a real appraisal).
  4. Take a photo of each room and of anything valuable. Make sure no IDs, bank statements, or credit cards are in the background.
  5. Label the box with the room and a number. Write the number next to the item in your inventory.
  6. Repeat room by room.

Room-by-room inventory checklist

Living room

  • Sofa and chairs
  • TV and entertainment center
  • Bookshelves and books
  • Rugs and lamps
  • Art and mirrors

Kitchen

  • Major appliances (fridge, stove, dishwasher — note if they stay or go)
  • Pots, pans, dishes, glassware
  • Small appliances (coffee maker, microwave, toaster)
  • Pantry food (unopened non-perishables only)

Bedrooms (each room)

  • Bed, mattress, dresser, nightstand
  • Clothes (group: “summer clothes — 3 boxes”)
  • Jewelry and valuables (keep these with you, not on the truck)
  • Electronics (TV, game console, computer)

Bathrooms

  • Medicine cabinet items (group: “toiletries — 1 box”)
  • Towels and linens
  • Scale, mirror

Garage, basement, attic

  • Tools and toolbox
  • Lawnmower, grill, outdoor gear
  • Holiday decorations
  • Storage bins (label each bin)

Everything else

  • Bikes, sports equipment
  • Musical instruments
  • Luggage
  • Important documents (keep with you — do not upload to AI)

Photos

  • One photo of each room, wide angle
  • Close-up photos of anything valuable or fragile
  • Photos of electronics with model numbers visible (useful for insurance)
  • Store photos in one folder: moving-inventory-photos

Insurance

If you hire movers, ask about their coverage and what it pays for damaged items. Your renters or homeowners insurance may also cover things in transit. Keep your inventory and photos together — they help if you ever need to file a claim. This is general information only, not insurance advice; talk to your agent or insurer about your policy.

Using AI with your inventory

AI can draft your list and suggest categories. Good prompts:

  • “Make a room-by-room inventory checklist for a two-bedroom apartment, one room per section, with checkboxes.”
  • “I’m packing box number 6 in the kitchen. Suggest a label format: room, box number, contents, fragile?”
  • “Write a short email I can send to my insurance agent asking what my renters policy covers during a move.”

Never paste serial numbers of valuables, real appraised values, receipts, or photos containing IDs into an AI chat.
